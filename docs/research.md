## 3. Menu findings

- **No verified individual menu items or prices for this café were obtainable.** The listing has no menu section, and every menu-carrying directory was unreachable (see §2).
- The photographed signboard appears to carry small sub-text of the kind "Coffee | Snacks | Fast Food", but it is **not legible** in the available photograph — recorded as [U] and **not reproduced as fact** on the website.
- Verified category facts used instead: the Google listing categorises the business as a **Coffee shop** and a **Tiffin center** (an Indian term for a venue serving daily home-style meals, typically thali/dabba service).
- **Consequence:** this website deliberately presents *what kind of food* the café serves (coffee/chai, tiffin meals, snacks) without inventing a single dish name or price, and points to a tap-to-call CTA for today's tiffin. This is an intentional honesty decision, not an omission.

## 4. Review themes

- The listing displays **5.0 stars**.
- Individual review text and the review count are **not accessible** in the anonymous "limited view" of Google Maps from this research environment (the reviews tab is not rendered). No review quotes are therefore used anywhere on the site — only the star value with a link to the live listing, letting visitors see reviews in context.
- Repeated complaint themes: **not researchable** (no accessible review text). None invented.

## 6. Existing online-presence assessment

- Google Maps listing only. No website, no social profiles discoverable, no food-delivery presence discoverable. Classic tiny-business discovery gap — the demo's reason for existing.

## 7. Opportunities for the website (all grounded in verified facts)

1. Lead with the **24 hours** fact — rare in this area.
2. Tap-to-call for **today's tiffin** (tiffin centers rotate daily; the phone is the real menu interface).
3. Directions straight to the correct Google listing.
4. Bilingual brand presence: the café's own Hindi name (ग्रीन हाउस कैफे) is part of its identity.
5. Show the real exterior — the green facade and fairy lights are the café's most distinctive, verified visual asset.

## 8. Unverified facts intentionally EXCLUDED from the website

- Any menu item name, any price, any "pure veg/non-veg" claim.
- Review count and review quotes.
- Founding date, owner name, staff, history, awards, "homemade" claims, ingredient claims.
- Instagram or any social handle.
- Any cuisine photograph other than the café's own listing photo (no stock food photography is used anywhere on this site — stock would misrepresent a specific real business).

## 9. Creative direction (grounded in the evidence above)

**"The green house after dark."** The single verified photograph is an evening shot of a green-meshed facade glowing with warm fairy lights under a yellow cursive sign — that is the café's real identity, and the design extends it rather than inventing one: deep botanical greens, warm amber/gold, cream paper, Fraunces (a warm, storybook serif that echoes the cursive signage) paired with Karla; a dark, atmospheric hero using the real photograph with a gentle warm gradient; restrained botanical line details; generous whitespace; the 24-hour story told quietly and confidently. No template gradients, no glassmorphism, no fake review widgets, no invented dishes. The site should feel like the café at 11 pm: small, warm, green, and open.
